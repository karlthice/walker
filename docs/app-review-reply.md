# Reply to App Review (Guideline 2.1 – Information Needed)

Submission `fd665784-caee-42c7-b70f-a6a8abbb2c6a`, rejected 19 Nov 2026 asking for more information.

Apple asks for the same text in two places: as a reply in App Store Connect, and in the
**Notes** field under App Review Information so later submissions have it too. The Notes field
holds 4,000 characters, and the reply below fits.

## Screen recording (make it on your iPhone)

Record on a physical iPhone running the latest iOS, starting from a fresh install so the
permission prompts appear. Use Control Center → Screen Recording. Aim for 1–2 minutes:

1. Delete Walker, install the TestFlight build and open it. Show the welcome screen, tap through
   it, and choose **Allow While Using App**, then **Change to Always Allow** when iOS asks.
2. Show the fog-covered map with your position cleared.
3. Walk (or drive) a short distance with the recording running, so the fog visibly lifts along
   the path. A cut in the middle is fine; just make sure the before and after are both shown.
4. Tap the raw-path button to show the path, then turn it off.
5. Tap the photo button, allow Photos access, and show a photo pin opening.
6. Open the **Stats** tab: today/week/month, then a country, then its city list.
7. Open Settings (gear): the background tracking toggle, Access section, and **Delete All Data**
   (show the confirmation; you can cancel it).

Attach the video to the reply. If App Store Connect rejects the file because it's too large, trim
it in Photos, or upload it to a private link (iCloud Drive, Google Drive) and paste the link.

## Text to paste

> Thank you for reviewing Walker. Here is the requested information; we have also added it to the App Review Notes.
>
> **1. Screen recording**
> Attached: a recording on a physical iPhone running the latest iOS, from a fresh install. It shows the welcome screen and location permission prompts, the fog clearing during a real walk, the raw path, photos on the map (with the Photos permission prompt), the Stats tab with country and city coverage, and Settings including Delete All Data.
> - Account registration, login and account deletion: not applicable. Walker has no accounts and no login. All data is stored only on the device and can be erased with Settings → Delete All Data.
> - User-generated content: not applicable. There is no sharing, no social features and no content from other users. The only photos shown are the user's own, read from their photo library on the device.
> - Paid content: not applicable. Walker is free, with no in-app purchases, subscriptions or ads.
>
> **2. Purpose and target audience**
> Walker is for anyone who walks, commutes or travels and is curious how much of their city, country or the world they have actually seen. The map starts covered in fog; wherever the user goes, the fog lifts street by street. Stats show new area explored per day, week and month, the share of each country explored, and the share of over 300 major cities. It turns everyday walks into exploration and nudges people to discover streets and neighbourhoods they have never visited. It is private by design: nothing leaves the iPhone.
>
> **3. Setup and main features**
> No login, credentials or sample files are needed.
> 1. Open the app. A welcome screen explains the fog and why "Always" location access is needed, then iOS asks for permission. Choose Allow While Using App, and Change to Always Allow when asked. Background location is the core feature: it clears the fog while the app is closed.
> 2. The Map tab shows the fog with the current position cleared. Move about 100 m or more (walking or driving) and the fog lifts along the path. On a fresh install there is little to see until the device has moved.
> 3. Map buttons: show the raw path (optionally faded by age) and show photos where they were taken (asks for Photos access).
> 4. The Stats tab shows explored area by day, week and month, by country, and by city.
> 5. The gear button opens Settings: pause background tracking, see access status, and Delete All Data.
>
> **4. External services**
> - Apple MapKit for the base map (Apple Maps tiles).
> - Apple Core Location for the user's position, and Apple PhotoKit to read photo locations. Both are on-device.
> Walker has no server of its own and uses no analytics, advertising, authentication, payment or AI services. Country and city boundaries are bundled in the app (Natural Earth, public domain; OpenStreetMap, ODbL), so no location is ever looked up online.
>
> **5. Regional differences**
> Walker works the same in all regions. The fog, map, path, photos and country stats work worldwide. The city list covers over 300 major cities around the world; places outside those cities still clear the fog and count toward country stats. The interface is in English.
>
> **6. Regulated industries and third-party material**
> Walker is not in a regulated industry. The only third-party material is bundled map data: country borders from Natural Earth (public domain) and city boundaries from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Both are credited in Settings → About, and OpenStreetMap is also credited under each city list.
